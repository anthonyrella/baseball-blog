Every fan has a field that made them fall in love with the game. For most of us, it wasn't a big-league ballpark. It was a patch of dirt with a backstop that leaned a little to the left.

There were no umpires, so we argued every close play. There weren't enough gloves, so we shared. And when someone hit one into Mrs. Henderson's yard, the game was over until somebody was brave enough to knock on her door.

## What we learned there

The sandlot taught us to choose sides fairly, to keep playing when we were losing, and to shake hands at the end — even with the kid who always called his own pitches.

Those lessons carried well beyond the diamond. Maybe that's why the stories from those summers still feel like the best ones.
