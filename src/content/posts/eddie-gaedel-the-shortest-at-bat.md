On August 19, 1951, the St. Louis Browns were playing a doubleheader against the Detroit Tigers. Between games, the Browns celebrated the American League's 50th anniversary, and a cake was wheeled onto the field. Out of it popped Eddie Gaedel, wearing a Browns uniform with the number **1/8** on the back.

Most of the crowd assumed that was the end of the gag. It wasn't.

## "Now batting for Saucier…"

In the bottom of the first inning of the second game, Browns manager Zack Taylor sent Gaedel up to pinch-hit. The umpire, Ed Hurley, objected — until Taylor produced a valid player contract. Owner Bill Veeck had thought of everything.

Gaedel stood all of 3 feet 7 inches tall. Crouched in his stance, his strike zone was said to be about an inch and a half. Tigers pitcher Bob Cain, laughing, threw four straight balls. Gaedel trotted to first base, was replaced by a pinch runner, and walked off into history.

> Veeck later wrote that he told Gaedel he'd be standing on the roof with a rifle if he so much as thought about swinging.

## Why it still matters

The league voided Gaedel's contract the next day, but his line in the record books still stands: one game, one plate appearance, one walk, and a perfect **1.000 on-base percentage**.

It's the kind of story that reminds us baseball has always had room for a little mischief.
