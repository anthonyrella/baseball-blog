On June 29, 1905, Archibald "Moonlight" Graham trotted out to right field for the New York Giants in the bottom of the eighth inning. He played the ninth, too — but when his turn in the order came around, the inning ended with him waiting on deck.

That was it. One game, no at-bats. He never appeared in the majors again.

## A different kind of career

Graham had already earned his medical degree, and he went on to serve for decades as a doctor in Chisholm, Minnesota, where he was much loved for his work with the town's schoolchildren.

Most fans know his name today thanks to W. P. Kinsella's novel *Shoeless Joe* and the film it inspired, *Field of Dreams*. But the real Graham's story — a sliver of a big-league career followed by a lifetime of quiet service — is every bit as moving as the fiction.

## The box score never tells the whole story

Baseball keeps careful records, but some of its best stories live in the margins. Moonlight Graham is proof that a single inning can echo for more than a century.
